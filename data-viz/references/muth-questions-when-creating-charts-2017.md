# What questions to ask when creating charts

- **Author:** Lisa Charlotte Muth (Datawrapper)
- **URL:** https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/better-charts
- **Date:** 2017-11-15
- **Good for:** Mid-article order of operations: point → proof on the figure → what the figure actually is.

Three steps, in this order. Changing the point means redoing the rest.

1. **What’s the point?** Why is this in the article? What should survive in memory? That sentence is the **headline** (a hypothesis, not a topic). “Cuts would hit New York, California the hardest,” not “iPhone sales.”
2. **How does the figure prove it?** Chart type is only the first lever. Then: **comparison data** (other products, other years, a baseline — without a comparison you have a number, not a viz); **grey + one highlight color**; **annotations** for the “why” the geometry cannot say.
3. **What does it show, exactly?** Subtitle with who/what/when/units; labels next to marks; **source**. Readers did not sit through the exploration.

“What should I compare it to?” is as important as “what chart type.”
