# What to consider when creating tables

- **Author:** Lisa Charlotte Muth (Datawrapper)
- **URL:** https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/guide-what-to-consider-when-creating-tables
- **Date:** 2019-05-21
- **Good for:** When the right “chart” is a table — and how not to dump a spreadsheet.

**Use a table when** readers will look up their row; numbers must be exact enough to act on; the values are things people already read as numbers (age, drink sizes); they need to compare **two directions** at once; or you are showing **ranks** (1 is not “half of 2”).

**Use a chart when** the job is the pattern, the outlier, or the ranking the eye should not have to hunt for. Long tables are read top-down; people quit before the interesting row.

Construction:

- Fewer columns; more rows than columns (vertical skim). Abbreviations, `1.3m`, icons.
- Sort for the story, not the alphabet. Pagination on long tables so the article below is not a surprise.
- Zebra stripes only when many columns would lose the row.
- Color as a pointer (a column, a party, min/max) — pastel fills, brighter type.
- Search/sort if people will hunt “their” city or firm.
- **Bars in the important numeric column** (hybrid). Heatmap cells if space is tight; one gradient per measure.
- Sparklines for a trend inside a row — default axes differ, so they are *not* comparable across rows. Need a shared scale → a real line chart.
