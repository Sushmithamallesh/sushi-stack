# What to consider when using text in data visualizations

- **Author:** Lisa Charlotte Muth (Datawrapper)
- **URL:** https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/text-in-data-visualizations
- **Date:** 2022-09-28
- **Good for:** Labels, titles, units, annotations — the words that *are* the chart.

Put the explanation where the eye already is.

- **Direct labels** beat a color key (museum labels next to the door). Default on line/pie/donut on desktop; mobile may have to fall back to a key.
- **Repeat the units** on axes, tooltips, and annotations — not only in the description. Prefer `20m` to “in millions” plus mental math.
- Tooltips should say *what* as well as *how much* (`3.4% unemployed`, not `3.4%`).
- Move **axis ticks** toward the data that matter (often the right edge of a bar chart).
- **Annotate** outliers, breaks in the series, and any mark you drew (highlight ranges, connectors). Annotations also make a chart worth entering.
- Readable type: what people already read on the web (sans, sentence case, ≥12px, near-black). If it “won’t fit,” shorten, tooltip, enlarge the chart, or hide the least important notes on mobile — don’t shrink into unreadable.
- Hierarchy: one loud title; two annotation sizes max; grey for the rest.
- **Left-align** titles and long notes. Centered blocks look messy and slow the next line.
- **Do not rotate** labels. Rephrase, wrap, or switch to bars.
- Outline type that sits on gridlines or marks.
- Phrase like a friend first, then get precise in the subtitle/notes. “Rents are rising” in the title; “change in rent, Q1 2020–Q1 2022, multi-family, counties with ≥1,000 units” below.
- Drop false precision (`22.42%`, `12,831`). Put exact values in a tooltip or the article if someone needs them.
