# Why I don’t like standardised effect sizes

- **Author:** Jan Vanhove
- **URL:** https://janhove.github.io/posts/2015-02-05-standardised-vs-unstandardised-es/
- **Date:** 2015-02-05
- **Good for:** Cohen’s *d* / Pearson *r* on a chart vs the original units.

Unstandardized: 7 cm taller; 4 ms slower per year. Standardized: those numbers divided by sample SDs.

Units people can think in beat dimensionless scores — including labelled Likert steps. Standardized sizes **inflate when you measure better or average repeated trials** (smaller SD, same mean difference) and **jump around with sampling** (extreme-groups designs boost *r*; restriction of range kills *r*; betas stay put). “Small/medium/large” yardsticks mix real effects with method and field maturity.

Mixed models and logistic models do not have a clean *R*² anyway — don’t invent one for the figure.

**For this skill:** default the axis to the measured unit. Use *d* or *r* only when the scale is junk *and* you say what SD it is. Never compare *d*s across studies as if they were the same object.
