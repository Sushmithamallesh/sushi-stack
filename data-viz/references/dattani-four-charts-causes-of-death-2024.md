# Four charts to understand causes of death across the lifespan

- **Author:** Saloni Dattani
- **URL:** https://www.scientificdiscovery.dev/p/four-charts-to-understand-causes
- **Date:** 2024-04-20
- **Good for:** Proof that one topic needs several figures. Metric × form pairs, each titled as its own question.

Walkthrough of US death-certificate data (2018–2021) under the too-broad question “How do causes of death vary with age?”

| Form | Metric | What it actually answers | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stacked bars | **Counts** | How many people die of each cause at each age; total deaths | Looks like risks “level off” because the living population shrinks |
| Small-multiple lines (own y-scales) | **Rates** | How risk from *this* cause changes with age | Hard to compare causes; tiny causes vanish if you force a shared scale |
| Stacked area/curve of rates | **Rates, stacked** | Risks of different causes *and* total risk at each age (works because causes are exclusive on a death certificate) | Age pattern *within* a cause gets lost; infancy is unreadable |
| 100% stacked | **Shares** | What people are dying *from* at that age | Makes cancers look like they decline after ~65; they don’t — CVD rises faster |

Counts depend on risk × how many people are alive. Plot the age structure if a count chart looks mysterious.

Stacking rates is usually queasy; here it is legitimate because each death has one underlying cause.

**For this skill:** if the question is still a topic, you do not have a chart yet. Title each figure with the narrow question it can survive. Prefer a series over a compromise graphic.
